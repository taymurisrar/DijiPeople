# QA-BILLING-048 — Bank-transfer settlement is authorized, atomic and single-allocation

## Preconditions

A disposable PostgreSQL database; two pending PKR bank orders with distinct
invoices; platform users holding read-only, general billing-management and the
dedicated payment-verification permissions; and one independently observed bank
deposit reference. Provider calls are faked and no money moves.

## Steps

1. As unauthenticated, read-only and general billing roles, try approve, reject,
   request-clarification, reconcile and refund actions. Repeat as the dedicated
   verifier.
2. Approve with zero, partial, excessive and wrong-currency received amounts;
   approve an expired and a cancelled order.
3. Approve the exact deposit once, retry the same request, then race five exact
   approvals from two authorized operators.
4. Allocate the same normalized bank transaction reference to the second order;
   vary case, whitespace and punctuation that normalization declares
   insignificant.
5. Race an exact bank approval with a Stripe/Safepay confirmation for the same
   purchase, and race two different exact payments for the same invoice.
6. Reject and request clarification twice; attempt approval from each terminal
   state. Request a refund twice and simulate provider/storage/audit failure at
   each transaction boundary.
7. Read payment, invoice, subscription-order, reconciliation, audit and outbox
   rows after every case.

## Expected Result

- Only the dedicated verification permission can decide a bank payment.
  `payments.read`/`payments.manage` do not silently inherit financial approval;
  REG-067 remains closed.
- Partial, excessive, wrong-currency, expired and cancelled cases never settle
  silently. They enter explicit reconciliation/refund/terminal states with no
  activation. Zero and negative received amounts are refused.
- Exactly one transaction claims the invoice/order and exactly one allocation
  owns a bank reference. Retries return the existing result; concurrent losers
  create no second payment, invoice credit, audit decision or outbox event.
- Provider and bank races credit the purchase once. A real surplus/duplicate is
  retained as a reconciliation/refund case rather than discarded or double
  charged.
- Each decision records actor, time, expected and received amount/currency,
  order/invoice/payment reference, reason and before/after state through the
  central audit service in the same transaction. Sensitive bank/evidence data
  is redacted.
- A failure before commit changes nothing; a failure after durable settlement
  is safely retryable and cannot reverse or repeat the allocation.

## Notes

Planned for TASK-0038 WP-07; not executed. This requires real PostgreSQL because
the assertion is on uniqueness, conditional writes, locks and transaction
rollback rather than on service call order.
