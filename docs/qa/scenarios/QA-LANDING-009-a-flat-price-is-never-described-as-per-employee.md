# QA-LANDING-009 — A flat price is never described as per-employee

## Preconditions

None. `billingUnitLabel` is a pure function over a resolved commercial offer.

## Steps

1. Render a `FLAT` monthly offer and read the unit label.
2. Render a `FLAT` annual offer.
3. Render a `PER_SEAT` monthly offer.
4. Render an unavailable offer, and `null`.

## Expected Result

1. `per month` — and no mention of employees, seats or users.
2. `per year`.
3. `per active employee / month`. The product sells both models: public plans
   are flat, and a negotiated per-seat price stays available through
   `PlanPrice.billingModel`, so the two answers must not collapse into one.
4. `null` in both cases. A price nobody can buy gets no unit.

## Notes

The `copy-contradicts-configuration` pattern, and the reason it survived so long
is instructive: **the arithmetic was never wrong.** `estimateCost` and
`calculateSeatPricing` both refuse to multiply a flat price, each with a comment
explaining why. Every number the customer saw was correct. Only the words around
them claimed a model the configuration did not use — the Terms, the billing
terms, the features page, the plans metadata, the plans hero and the estimator
all said "per active employee".

`billingUnitLabel` returning `null` for a flat price is what tipped it from
sloppy to misleading: the figure rendered with no unit at all, directly beneath
a heading saying the price was per person, leaving the reader to do a
multiplication nobody would ever charge them for.

Worth keeping in mind when judging severity here: the error overstated the
price. A 500-person company read `$199 per active employee` and concluded
DijiPeople cost $99,500 a month. The cost of this defect was in sales that never
happened, which is the kind that leaves no trace in any log.
