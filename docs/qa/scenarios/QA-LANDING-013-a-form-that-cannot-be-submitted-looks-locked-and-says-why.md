# QA-LANDING-013 — A form that cannot be submitted looks locked and says why

## Preconditions

A plan whose selected price is **not** checkout-ready — in practice any plan in
an environment where Stripe is not fully configured. Platform Admin access for
the operator half.

## Steps

1. `npm --workspace landing run test -- subscribe-lock`
2. Open `/subscribe` and select that plan.
3. Try to type into Company name.
4. Read the area immediately above the fields.
5. Change the plan selector to one that **is** checkout-ready.
6. In Platform Admin, open the plan and find the price that is not
   checkout-ready. Read its badge **without hovering**.

## Expected Result

Step 3: the fields do not accept input, and they visibly look inert — reduced
contrast and a not-allowed cursor, not an ordinary-looking form that ignores you.

Step 4: a panel states that the form is locked, why, that the plan and billing
selectors above are still live, and offers a link to contact.

Step 5: the form unlocks immediately without a reload.

Step 6: every readiness reason is listed on the page as text.

## Notes

The form being disabled is correct and deliberate — BUG-0066 and BUG-0082 exist
because the wizard once collected five steps of organization profile, owner
identity and signed agreements before revealing a dead submit button. This
scenario is not about whether it locks; it is about whether a person can tell
that it did, and why.

Considerations:

- **Exactly one** element may carry `subscribe-unavailable-notice`. The BUG-0066
  browser journey locates it and asserts visibility, so a duplicate is a
  strict-mode violation. If you add a second copy of the message anywhere,
  it must be id-less.
- Step 5 is the one that proves the plan selectors were deliberately left
  outside the disabled fieldset. Disabling those would replace one dead end with
  a worse one.
- Step 6 is the other end of "contact us and we will arrange it". If an operator
  cannot see which of the ten readiness conditions failed, that sentence leads
  nowhere.
- The automated half is structural, over the component source, because
  `apps/landing` jest runs in node with no jsdom. It cannot observe contrast or
  a cursor; steps 3–6 are the real verification.
