# QA-BILLING-009 — A confirmed payment provisions exactly one workspace automatically

## Preconditions

A `SubscriptionOrder` in `PENDING_PAYMENT` with a `requestedSlug` and a customer
carrying owner contact details. Real PostgreSQL.

## Steps

1. Confirm payment for the order, so `PAYMENT_CONFIRMED` is emitted and the
   outbox opens an onboarding and emits `PROVISIONING_REQUESTED`.
2. Let the dispatcher run.
3. Read the tenant, the order and the customer account.
4. Redeliver `PROVISIONING_REQUESTED` and repeat step 3.
5. Separately: empty the `handles` array on `ProvisioningRequestedHandler` and
   run `emitted-events-have-consumers.invariant.spec.ts`.

## Expected Result

Exactly one `Tenant` exists, its slug is the reserved `requestedSlug`, and
`Tenant.customerAccountId` equals `SubscriptionOrder.customerAccountId`. The
order carries `tenantId` and `activatedAt`, and its `requestedSlug` is released.
No human action was required at any point.

Step 4 changes nothing — no second tenant, owner, membership or subscription.

Step 5 **fails**, naming `PROVISIONING_REQUESTED`. That is the assertion that
matters: the defect was an event nobody listened to, and a check that only ever
passes cannot prove somebody is listening now.

## Notes

`AUTOMATION_STATUS: PARTIAL`, stated honestly. Steps 1–4 exercise the handler and
the engine directly; a true end-to-end run from a Stripe webhook needs live
provider credentials this environment does not have (TASK-0007 OD-03). Step 5 is
fully automated and is the part that guards the regression.

The `declared-but-unwired-step` pattern — the same shape as BUG-0014, where a
provisioning step declared itself retryable and was never wired.
