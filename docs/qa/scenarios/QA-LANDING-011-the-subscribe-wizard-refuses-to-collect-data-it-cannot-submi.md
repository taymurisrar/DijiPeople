# QA-LANDING-011 — The subscribe wizard refuses to collect data it cannot submit

## Preconditions

A published plan whose resolved price for the visitor's currency and interval is
**not** checkout-ready, and a plan/currency/interval combination that resolves no
price at all. Both are reachable today: the seeded PKR schedule is a placeholder
and no QAR prices exist, so a visitor in Qatar meets the second case.

## Steps

Unit — `apps/landing/lib/plans.spec.ts`:

1. `checkoutBlockedReason` on a purchasable price returns null.
2. On a price that resolves but is not checkout-ready, it returns a sentence
   naming that reason and offering a way forward.
3. On no price at all, it returns a *different* sentence. The two must not
   collapse into one — "no price for your region" and "price exists but is not
   wired to Stripe" send the reader to different places.
4. Its answer agrees with `isCheckoutReady` in both directions.

Browser — `e2e/tests/flow-c-landing-public-surface.spec.ts`:

5. Open `/subscribe` with an unpurchasable selection.
6. `#subscribe-unavailable-notice` is visible.
7. The step `<fieldset>` is disabled and no input inside it is interactive.
8. **`Continue` is disabled**, not only the submit button.
9. The plan and billing selectors outside the fieldset remain interactive.

## Expected Result

The visitor learns on the first step that this plan cannot be bought, and can
switch to one that can. They are never able to type an organization profile, an
owner identity and two accepted agreements towards a submit button that will not
fire.

Step 8 is the one that carries this scenario. The submit button lives only on
the wizard's final step, so an assertion about submit cannot see the defect —
by the time submit exists, all the typing has already happened. Any future
rewrite of this screen must keep the assertion on `Continue`.

## Notes

[[BUG-0082]] is [[BUG-0066]] returning. The first fix was structural — a
disabled `<fieldset>` and an id on a paragraph — and it did not survive WP-11's
rewrite from a single page into five steps: the fields were kept and everything
around them was replaced.

The fix this scenario guards moves the rule into `checkoutBlockedReason`, one
named function returning the visitor-facing sentence or null, consumed by the
notice, the fieldset and `Continue` alike. A named function is harder to drop in
a rewrite than a wrapper element, and a unit test can hold it without a browser.
