# QA-LEGAL-001 — A legal document that declares itself a draft is never published

## Preconditions

None. Pure functions over document text.

## Steps

1. Run `findDraftSelfDeclarations` over the literal banner the seeded documents
   carry.
2. Run `findUnfilledPlaceholders` over the same text.
3. Run both over a document that is genuinely ready.
4. Run both over a terms of service that merely *discusses* draft contracts.
5. Run over text containing `TODO` / `TBD`.

## Expected Result

1. Flagged, on three independent signals.
2. **Empty** — and this is the diagnosis, not a redundant assertion.
3. and 4. Not flagged.
5. Flagged as an unfinished-work marker.

## Notes

On 2026-08-22 all ten legal documents were published to production carrying a
banner reading *"Draft — not published, and not legal advice … It has not been
reviewed by a lawyer."* The privacy policy of a live product told its readers not
to rely on it.

**Nothing refused, and step 2 is why.** `findUnfilledPlaceholders` was the only
content gate, and it asks whether the template was *filled in*. The documents
answered that perfectly — complete prose, no placeholders. They simply said they
were drafts. A gate that asks the wrong question passes confidently, which is the
failure mode here rather than a missing gate.

Step 4 is the false positive that would get this check deleted. A published terms
of service may legitimately talk about draft contracts and draft statements of
work; a guard that refuses over the bare word "draft" gets bypassed, and then it
protects nothing.

The patterns match against **flattened** text. The banner is markdown
hard-wrapped inside a blockquote, so "not been reviewed by a
> lawyer" is split
across a line break — the first version of the guard matched the raw string and
found nothing, which is the same shape of failure it exists to prevent.
