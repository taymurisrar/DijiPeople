# QA-LANDING-023 — Checkout quotes the same price and basis the plans page advertises

## Preconditions

A market with at least one purchasable plan. The interesting case is a plan
whose catalogue holds more than one billing model for the same currency and
interval — with only one model present, the two pages agree whether or not the
rule is applied.

## Steps

1. Open `/plans` and read a plan's price and its basis, on both the Monthly and
   Annual toggles.
2. Click that plan's CTA through to `/subscribe`.
3. Read the price and basis on the "Selected plan" card.
4. Change the **Billing** select on `/subscribe` and read it again.
5. Multiply out: does the seat estimate equal the advertised unit price times
   the seat count?

## Expected Result

- **Steps 1–4** — the same amount and the same basis on both pages. "QAR 8 per
  active employee / month" on `/plans` is "QAR 8" and a per-seat estimate on
  `/subscribe`, never "QAR 249, billed as one subscription."
- **Step 5** — the arithmetic is consistent with the advertised unit price.
- The seat minimum offered by the wizard is the *published* model's minimum.
  Flat and per-seat differ here — 1 against 10 for QAR Starter — so the wrong
  one opens the wizard on a seat count the real price will reject.

The price a visitor is quoted is identified by three things: currency, billing
cycle and billing **model**. Any resolution matching fewer than three depends on
the order the API happens to return prices in, which is not a contract.

## Notes

Created 2026-08-25 at `b926608a`, from the fix for [[BUG-1369]].

Partially automated: `apps/landing/lib/plans.spec.ts` and
`subscribe-selection.spec.ts` cover the resolution, and the fixture lists the
FLAT price **first** because that is the order production returned — a fixture
with per-seat first would pass against the broken resolver and prove nothing.
The cross-page comparison in steps 1–4 is the manual half. Guards `REG-260`.

Root cause was one layer down: [[BUG-1378]], where `/public/plans` published
internal `SALES_ASSISTED` pricing that the wizard then had to choose among.
[[QA-BILLING-021]] guards that. Both are worth running — one stops internal
prices reaching the client, the other stops the client choosing wrongly among
whatever it is given.
