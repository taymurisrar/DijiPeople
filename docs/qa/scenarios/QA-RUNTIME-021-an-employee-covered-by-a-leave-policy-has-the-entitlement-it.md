# QA-RUNTIME-021 — An employee covered by a leave policy has the entitlement it grants

## Preconditions

- A tenant with at least two employees.
- A leave policy granting a known number of annual days, assigned tenant-wide.
- A second, more specific policy — EMPLOYEE-scoped — granting a *different*
  number to one of those employees.
- The annual leave type left at its default `consumesBalance: true`.

## Steps

1. `npm --workspace api run test -- leave-entitlement`
2. Create or edit the tenant-wide policy assignment.
3. Read `LeaveBalance` for both employees.
4. Submit a leave request within entitlement for each. Submit one beyond it.
5. Deactivate the specific assignment and re-read the second employee's balance.

## Expected Result

- Step 1: six assertions pass, the first being that allocation follows the
  policy that **wins** rather than the one just assigned.
- Step 3: employee one has the tenant policy's entitlement; employee two has the
  **specific** policy's, not the tenant one. `totalRemaining` equals
  `totalAllocated` minus `totalUsed` for both.
- Step 4: the in-entitlement requests pass the balance gate. The over-entitlement
  one is refused with "Insufficient leave balance for this request." — unless the
  rule allows a negative balance.
- Step 5: employee two is recomputed to the tenant policy's entitlement, because
  the specific assignment no longer wins.

## Notes

**Step 3 on employee two is the whole scenario.** Before the fix every balance
was zero and every request was refused, so any employee would demonstrate the
bug. What a *second* employee demonstrates is the thing most likely to be got
wrong in the fix: the assignment is the trigger, not the source. Exactly one
policy governs an employee, by specificity — EMPLOYEE beats DEPARTMENT beats
TENANT — so allocating the triggering assignment's entitlement to everyone it
covers would write a number that no policy applying to employee two justifies,
and the gate would then enforce it. That is a worse defect than the one being
fixed, because it is silently wrong rather than uniformly blocking.

The automated half mutation-tests exactly this: an implementation that resolves
the policy once instead of per employee passes five of the six assertions.

**Steps 2 to 5 are not runnable end to end today.** Submitting a leave request
also needs a routable approval chain, and on a tenant with the shipped seed there
isn't one — see BUG-1968 and QA-RUNTIME-017. Steps 3 and 5 can be checked by
reading `LeaveBalance` directly without submitting anything, and that is the
useful subset until then.

**Existing tenants are not backfilled.** Balances carrying `totalAllocated = 0`
are corrected on the next assignment write, so a tenant nobody touches stays
broken. That was a deliberate decision — a backfill rewrites balances on live
tenants, including one deliberately configured around this bug — and it is
recorded on BUG-1967 rather than assumed.
