# QA-REPORTING-011 — A workspace that cannot send email says so before a schedule promises one

## Preconditions

- A tenant whose only enabled email provider is `CONSOLE` or `DEV`, and whose
  platform relay is also unconfigured or a sink. The demo tenant is exactly this
  and the owner has decided to leave it so — **do not "fix" it by configuring a
  provider**, that is explicitly out of scope and would destroy the fixture.
- A second tenant with a working `SMTP` provider, or a platform relay configured
  with one, to check the negative case.
- An account holding `reports.write`, which is what the capability route
  requires — deliberately not the notification-admin permission that guards
  `GET /notifications/email-providers`, because the person creating a schedule
  will not hold that.

## Steps

1. On the **sink tenant**, open **Reports → Scheduled**.
2. Open a report in the runner and click **Schedule**.
3. Create the schedule and let it run, or trigger any templated email on that
   tenant.
4. Read the schedule's own status, then read the delivery log row for that send.
5. On the **delivering tenant**, repeat steps 1 and 2.
6. Set `LOG_LEVEL=info` on a production-mode service and read the boot output and
   an informational log line. Then set `LOG_LEVEL=chatty` and read the boot
   output.

## Expected Result

1. An amber notice above the list: "This workspace is not set up to send email."
It says the reports are still built and stored, does not use the word "failed",
and points at Settings → Email. It appears above the whole list, not against one
row — it is a fact about the workspace, including for schedules already running
that nobody has been receiving.

2. The same notice inside the dialog, above the fields, **and the form still
works**. Creating the schedule is reasonable: the report is produced either way
and will start arriving once a provider is configured. What must not happen is
someone promising themselves a daily email and finding out a day later.

3–4. The delivery log records **`NOT_DELIVERED`**, not `SENT`. `providerType`
is `CONSOLE`. The schedule's own `lastRunStatus` is still `COMPLETED` and
`lastFailureReason` still null — **this is correct and is the point**: nothing
failed, the report was built, and a sink must not make schedules fail and
auto-disable after `MAX_CONSECUTIVE_FAILURES`. The service log carries a
**warn**-level line saying the message was handed to a sink and not delivered.

5. **No notice at all**, on either surface, and the delivery log for a send on
that tenant still records `SENT`. A tenant whose delivery comes from the platform
relay rather than its own provider must also see no notice — the capability is
resolved through the same chain a real send walks.

6. `LOG_LEVEL=info` behaves as `log`: informational lines are emitted, and no
warning is produced. `LOG_LEVEL=chatty` still falls back to the production
default **and** emits one warning naming the accepted values.

## Notes

Created 2026-08-31 at `2b001494`.

The delivery-status, capability and log-level assertions are automated in
`services/api/src/modules/notifications/email/email-sink-delivery-status.spec.ts`,
`services/api/src/modules/notifications/email/email-delivery-capability.spec.ts`
and `services/api/src/log-level-resolution.spec.ts`.

**The manual part is steps 1, 2 and 5** — that the notice actually renders where
someone will read it, and stays away when the workspace can send.

A warning about how to re-run this: the whole defect existed because a green
pipeline was read as a delivered email. Assert on the delivery log row and the
absence of the message, never on the schedule reporting `COMPLETED`.
