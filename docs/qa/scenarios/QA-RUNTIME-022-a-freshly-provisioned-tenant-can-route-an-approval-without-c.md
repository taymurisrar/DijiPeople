# QA-RUNTIME-022 — A freshly provisioned tenant can route an approval without configuring anything

## Preconditions

A tenant provisioned through `provisionTenantForCustomer`, whose owner has
accepted their invitation and signed in at least once. Nothing else configured:
no reporting lines, no HR role members.

## Steps

1. `npm --workspace api run test -- default-approval-matrices`
2. Provision a tenant. Activate the owner account.
3. As an employee of that tenant, submit a leave request.
4. Open Settings > Approvals & Workflows > Approval Matrices.

## Expected Result

- Step 1: eight assertions pass, including that **every** active seeded step is
  bindable on a fresh tenant.
- Step 3: the request is accepted and routes to the tenant administrator. Before
  the change it was refused — the seeded chain required a reporting manager and
  an HR role holder, and a new tenant has neither.
- Step 4: the line-manager and HR steps are present and **inactive**, visible as
  a template to switch on once reporting lines and the HR role are populated.

## Notes

**Satisfiable is not satisfied, and step 2 is where that bites.** Provisioning
creates the owner with status `INVITED`, and `findActiveUsersByRoleId` requires
`ACTIVE` — so the seeded chain begins routing when the owner accepts their
invitation, not the instant the tenant exists. That is still a real improvement:
signing in is a precondition of using the product at all, whereas building a
reporting hierarchy and hiring into an HR role are not. Do not read step 3 as a
claim that leave works before anyone has logged in. BUG-1969 and ITEM-0106 are
the invited-approver question itself and are unchanged.

**The automated assertion is stricter than "the chain works".** It requires every
active step to be bindable, not merely one of them — because every matched step
must bind or the whole submission is refused (BUG-1968). The old seed had a
plausible sequence 1 and an unbindable sequence 2, and the whole chain was dead.

`system-admin` is the only role this may rely on: `provisionTenantForCustomer`
assigns it to the owner and throws if it cannot. If that ever changes, the test
list `ROLES_PROVISIONING_GUARANTEES` must change with it — and shortening the
test by adding a role provisioning does not actually populate would hide exactly
the defect this exists to catch.
