# QA-PLATFORM-030 — Expected protocol outcomes stay out of the triage queue and defects stay in it

## Preconditions

None. `isExpectedProtocolOutcome` is a pure function.

## Steps

Assert as routine: a `401` from each of `AUTH_TOKEN_MISSING`,
`AUTH_TOKEN_INVALID`, `AUTH_REFRESH_TOKEN_INVALID`, `AUTH_UNAUTHORIZED`,
`SESSION_EXPIRED` and `SESSION_REVOKED`; a `404` with `unmatchedRoute`; and a
`404 TENANT_NOT_FOUND` on the public host-resolution path, at three spellings
(with query string, with the `/api` prefix, and bare).

Then assert as still queued: a `400 VALIDATION_FAILED`; a `404` for a record
rather than a route; a `401 AUTH_INVALID_CREDENTIALS`; `403`, `409`, `422`,
`429`, `500`, `502`, `503`; an empty input; an unrecognised code; a
`TENANT_NOT_FOUND` with **no** path and one on an authenticated tenant route;
a `500` on the resolution path; and a path that merely starts with the
resolution prefix.

## Expected Result

The routine set is `NOT_AN_INCIDENT`; everything else is `NEW`.

The negative half is the scenario. This rule is a filter over the only queue
anyone watches, so every widening of it hides something: a `400` is frequently
our own frontend asking for something impossible (BUG-1742 presented exactly
that way), a spike in refused logins or denied permissions is something an
operator should see, and `TENANT_NOT_FOUND` on an authenticated route means a
tenant that should exist and does not.

## Why This Is Reusable

The rule has been widened twice — BUG-1754 created it, BUG-2465 added
`SESSION_REVOKED` and host resolution — and it will be widened again, because
the queue fills with whatever it does not recognise. Each widening must be
matched by a negative assertion, or the filter quietly becomes a blindfold.
Re-run on any change to `SESSION_AUTH_CODES` or `HOST_RESOLUTION_PATHS`.
