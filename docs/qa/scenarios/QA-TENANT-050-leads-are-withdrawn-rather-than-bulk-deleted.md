# QA-TENANT-050 — One deletion rule, for one record and for many

## Preconditions

- Platform Admin, signed in as a `PLATFORM_OWNER`.
- Several leads, at least one of them converted.
- At least one invoice, for the retention half.

## Steps

1. Run `npm --workspace api run test -- generic-delete`.
2. Open **Leads**, select several rows, and use the bulk delete action.
3. Open one lead and delete it from its record page.
4. `DELETE /api/super-admin/leads` with a list of ids, directly.
5. Open **Invoices**, select several rows, and look at the delete control.
6. Check the error log for any `Bulk delete is not available for this module`.

## Expected Result

- The suite passes: every runtime module answers identically through
  single-record delete and through bulk delete.
- Step 2 confirms first — naming how many records and which ones — and then
  deletes them.
- Step 3 still works. One record and a selection are the same decision now, not
  two.
- Step 4 succeeds for a platform administrator.
- Step 5 offers a **disabled** control carrying its retention reason. Invoices,
  payments, commissions, executed agreements, signature evidence, subscriptions,
  plans, templates, monitoring incidents and tenants all behave this way, in
  both directions.
- Step 6 finds nothing.

## Notes

**This scenario was inverted on 2026-08-28, the day it was written.** Its first
version asserted that bulk lead delete did *not* exist — the disposition the
repository owner took that morning, on the argument that a lead carries the
partner attribution a commission is calculated from. They reversed it hours
later after a production 400: an operator selected leads, pressed Delete, and
was refused, while deleting the same leads one at a time worked.

The attribution argument was not refuted, and it is answered elsewhere rather
than discarded: deletion is audited, the confirmation names the count and the
records, and converted leads are still refused individually by the service.

Step 6 is the one that catches the real defect, which was never really about
leads. `remove` and `bulkDelete` were two switch statements over the same
modules, and a module could be reachable through one and not the other. They
share one method now. The suite behind step 1 is written so that the *easy*
wrong fix — adding a missing arm to a second list — does not satisfy it.

Authorization changed with it: deleting anything, one or many, requires the
module's write permission **and** a platform admin role. That narrows
single-record delete for the presales roles, which is deliberate.
