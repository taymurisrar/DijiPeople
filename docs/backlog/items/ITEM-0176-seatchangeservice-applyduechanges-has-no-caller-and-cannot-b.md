# ITEM-0176 — SeatChangeService.applyDueChanges has no caller and cannot be wired without under-billing

## Summary

`SeatChangeService.applyDueChanges()` has no caller anywhere in the running
application. A scheduled seat change is written, stored, and never applied.

This is the same defect [[BUG-3331]] found and fixed in `PlanChangeService`, in
the service next door. It was deliberately left alone there, because the obvious
fix is worse than the defect.


## Why It Matters

Wiring it into the sweeper that now runs plan changes would activate a separate,
pre-existing gap: a scheduled seat **decrease** reduces the local purchased-seat
count with no matching quantity update at the payment provider. Running that
unattended would silently under-bill a tenant, every cycle, with no error and
nothing on any screen to show it.

So the current state is a feature that quietly does nothing, and the one-line
fix converts it into a revenue leak that nobody would notice. That is why this
carries an ExecPlan rather than a patch.

A seat decrease is also the direction a customer asks for, which means the
failure mode favours the customer and hides from the business — the kind that
survives a long time before anyone reports it.


## Evidence

- `SeatChangeService.applyDueChanges()` — no caller in the running application.
  Found while wiring the equivalent plan-change method into
  `SubscriptionChangeSweeperWorker` under [[BUG-3331]].
- The sweeper deliberately does **not** call it. The reason is recorded in the
  worker's own comment and in the `SUBSCRIPTION_CHANGE_SWEEPER_ENABLED` entry in
  `docs/environment-variables.md`, so the omission reads as a decision rather
  than an oversight.
- [[BUG-2618]] is the same shape of defect — a service method with no caller —
  and is worth reading for how it was found and what it cost.


## Proposed Approach

Needs an ExecPlan under [`PLANS.md`](../../PLANS.md): it moves money, and the
safe order of operations is the whole question.

The dependency comes first. A seat change must push the new quantity to the
payment provider in the same operation that records it locally, the way the
plan-change path now does. Until that holds, applying due seat changes is not
safe at any schedule.

Then decide what happens when the provider and the local record disagree — on a
decrease especially, since that is where the under-billing sits. The plan-change
path's answer is to report a pending sync rather than roll back a recorded
change, and the same reasoning probably applies, but it should be stated rather
than inherited.

Only then wire it into the sweeper, behind the same flag, off by default.

Worth auditing the other `applyDueChanges`-shaped methods in this module at the
same time. Two have now been found with no caller; a third would stop being a
coincidence.


## Acceptance Criteria

- A scheduled seat change applies at its due date, or is explicitly documented
  as not yet applied with the reason.
- A seat decrease updates the quantity at the payment provider and the local
  record together, or fails loudly enough that an operator sees it.
- No path exists in which a tenant's local seat count falls below what the
  provider is billing without a durable record of the divergence.
- A test fails if `applyDueChanges` loses its caller again.
- The audit of sibling methods is recorded, whatever it finds.
