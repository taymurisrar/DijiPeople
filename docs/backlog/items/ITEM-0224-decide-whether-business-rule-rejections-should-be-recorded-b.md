# ITEM-0224 — Decide whether business-rule rejections should be recorded but not queued as incidents

## Summary

About 70 NEW incidents in the production queue are business-rule rejections.
The product behaved correctly and told the user why, for example:

- insufficient leave balance
- already checked in
- approving one's own correction
- on-site-only work mode
- onboarding prerequisites not met
- contract required fields missing
- bulk-delete refusals

They are kept in the queue on purpose, under the [[BUG-1754]] rationale that a
rejection might hide a real defect. Nobody reviews them, so they only bury the
live incidents. Decide whether to add a "domain rule" class that is recorded but
not queued.

## Why It Matters

Business-rule rejections make up roughly a third of the queue's NEW incidents.
A real fault on the same endpoints is hard to see among them, and the queue
cannot work as an alerting surface.

## Evidence

Production `ErrorLog`, read-only query on 2026-10-07: about 70 NEW incidents
with about 110 occurrences, from 2026-08-10 to 2026-09-24, 1 occurrence in the
last 14 days, across 2 tenants.

## Proposed Approach

The options:

1. Keep queuing them, and review them once and mark them `NOT_AN_INCIDENT` as
   part of [[ITEM-0222]].
2. Add a catalog flag (`domainRule`) for business-rule error codes. Their rows
   are recorded, searchable and counted, but are never queued as NEW. A spike
   can still be alerted on by rate.
3. Stop recording them.

Option 2 keeps the evidence that [[BUG-1754]] wanted while restoring the queue's
signal. If chosen, it is a small catalog and classifier change.

## Acceptance Criteria

- The decision is recorded in this item.
- If option 2 is chosen: catalog codes are marked, rows with those codes are not
  queued, and they remain searchable.
