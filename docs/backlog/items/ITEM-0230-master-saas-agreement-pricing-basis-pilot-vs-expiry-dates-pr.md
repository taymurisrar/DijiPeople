# ITEM-0230 — Master SaaS agreement: pricing basis, pilot vs expiry dates, provider legal identity, co-signer party model

## Summary

Reviewing the executed `CON-20261012-E7421CC1` surfaced four questions that
are business or legal decisions, not defects. The code renders what was
entered; none of them was changed without the owner.

1. **Price basis.** Schedule A prints "Agreed Subscription Price / Rate: PKR
   6,000.00" with billing cycle MONTHLY, while clause 8.2 licenses by Active
   Employee. The document does not say whether PKR 6,000 is per employee per
   month, a flat monthly fee for the 20 licensed employees, or the pilot
   total.
2. **Pilot end vs agreement expiry.** Pilot 15 Oct 2026 – 15 Jan 2027
   ("3 months"); agreement expiry 30 Jan 2027, entered by hand; initial and
   renewal term 12 months by default. The 15-day gap equals the termination
   notice period but nothing in the document says so.
3. **Provider legal identity.** "DijiPeople Technologies", signatory "Founder &
   Proprietor", address only — no registration or tax number, while the
   customer carries both. Whether the provider is a sole proprietorship and
   which identifiers it must state is a legal question.
4. **Co-signer party model.** The third signer was added as a separate
   CUSTOMER party named "Anou" with no legal name. The renderer now treats a
   non-primary party of the same type as signing *for* the primary party
   ("Legal Contracting Party: DijiPeople Demo Company"). If a second party
   should instead be its own contracting party, it needs a legal name.

Also observed, data rather than code: the primary customer signatory's title
is "Taimur" (a name, not a capacity); the first two recipients were recorded
under party names ("DijiPeople", "DijiPeople Demo Company") — the dialog now
prefills the named signatory instead (BUG-4192).

## Decisions (owner, 2026-10-13)

1. **Price basis** — PKR 6,000 is per Active Employee per month. New term
   `commercial.priceBasis` defaults to "per Active Employee per month";
   template v4 prints it after the agreed price.
2. **Pilot end vs expiry** — the 30 January expiry was an entry mistake that
   nothing validated. Sending (and the document-fields view) now refuses an
   expiry that differs from the pilot end, a pilot starting before the
   agreement, and a pilot ending before it starts (`agreement-terms.ts`).
3. **Provider identity** — the provider may contract as a company or as an
   individual and holds neither identifier today. Settings → Company profile
   gains Entity type and Proprietor name; new `platform.legalIdentity` reads
   "<legal name>[, a company registered under number <n>]" or "<proprietor>,
   an individual trading as <legal name>". Template v4 uses it.
4. **Co-signer** — both options. Each non-primary party has "Signs as":
   Additional signatory (signs for the primary party of its type) or Separate
   party (its own contracting party and signature caption).
5. **Signatory title** — "Taimur" was entered by mistake; no change.
## Why It Matters

An executed agreement whose commercial unit is ambiguous is a dispute waiting
to happen; the price clause is the one most likely to be contested.

## Evidence

Production, read-only, contract `09a7718c-…`: placeholder values
`commercial.agreedPrice` = 6000, `commercial.billingCycle` = MONTHLY,
`commercial.pilotEndDate` = 2027-01-15, `contract.expiryDate` = 2027-01-30,
`contract.initialTerm` = 12 months, `platform.legalName` = DijiPeople
Technologies.

## Proposed Approach

Owner decides each point. Likely template changes (a price-basis term such as
"per Active Employee per month", a stated relationship between pilot end and
expiry, provider identifiers) go through the template draft → publish
workflow, never by editing a published version.

## Acceptance Criteria

Each of the four points has a recorded decision, and any resulting template
change is published as a new template version.
