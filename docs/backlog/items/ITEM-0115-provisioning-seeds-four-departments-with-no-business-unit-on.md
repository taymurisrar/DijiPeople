# ITEM-0115 — Provisioning seeds four departments with no business unit on every tenant

## Summary

`seedTenantWorkforceReferenceData` upserts `DEFAULT_DEPARTMENTS` — Human
Resources, Operations, Finance, Information Technology — with no
`businessUnitId`. It runs from `seed:config`, which `release:api` executes on
every deploy for every tenant, so these four rows exist on every tenant in the
product rather than only on demo data. Whether a provisioned department *should*
carry a business unit is a product question nobody has answered, and this item
exists to answer it rather than to fix a defect.

## Why It Matters

This was the invisible writer behind [[BUG-1957]]. Until that fix, a department
with no business unit could not be listed, opened, edited or deleted while still
holding its name against the tenant's uniqueness constraint — and the rows
reaching that state were not user mistakes, they were the ones provisioning
created. The read path is now fixed, so nothing is currently broken.

The cost of leaving it is narrower but real: every tenant starts with four
departments sitting outside its business-unit hierarchy, so they are invisible
to any BUSINESS_UNIT-scoped role and appear only to a caller whose effective
level is TENANT. An HR manager scoped to a business unit does not see the
department they were expected to work in, and nothing explains why.

## Evidence

- `services/api/prisma/seed-config.ts:1222-1239` — the upsert, with no
  `businessUnitId` in either the create or update branch.
- `services/api/prisma/seed-config.ts:56-61` — the `DEFAULT_DEPARTMENTS` list.
- `services/api/src/modules/organization/organization.service.ts:877-920` — the
  read path as fixed by [[BUG-1957]], which now admits these rows at tenant
  level.
- Found by the organization stream of SESSION-0076 while fixing [[BUG-1957]];
  the bug record could not name the writer, and this is it.

## Proposed Approach

Three directions, and the choice is a product one:

1. **Leave it.** Provisioned departments are tenant-wide by design, and a
   tenant assigns them to business units when it creates them. Cheapest, and
   consistent with a tenant that has no business units at provisioning time.
2. **Assign at provisioning.** Requires choosing a business unit, which a tenant
   may not have when `seed:config` first runs — so it needs a defined default
   business unit, which is itself new product surface.
3. **Stop seeding departments.** Let the tenant create its own. Changes
   onboarding for every new tenant and would orphan the rows already seeded.

No ExecPlan is needed to decide. Option 2 or 3 would need one before
implementation, because both touch provisioning on a live multi-tenant deploy.

## Acceptance Criteria

- A decision is recorded as an ADR naming which of the three directions is taken
  and why.
- If the decision is option 1, `DEFAULT_DEPARTMENTS` carries a comment stating
  that the absent business unit is deliberate, so the next reader does not file
  this again.
- If it is option 2 or 3, an ExecPlan exists covering the migration path for
  tenants already carrying the seeded rows.
